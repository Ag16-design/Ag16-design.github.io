MY TROUBLESHOOTING CASE · LC-MS/MS

From Low Recovery to Evidence-Based Decisions

How I separated matrix effects from extraction efficiency, found the main loss stage, and then improved the conditions and checked them across different food matrices. Each experiment answered a clear question and helped me decide what to test next.

Shimadzu LC-MS/MS 8050Shimadzu LC-MS/MS 8060Cross-matrix validation: 8050Peak Peak Peak Area · Recovery · CV

Summary

When I analyzed glycyrrhizic acid in food, the original sample preparation method gave a very low recovery. The initial method used two extractions with 50% methanol, but recovery in the plain steamed bun matrix was below the 70–120% acceptance range used in this study.

Increasing the number of extractions, changing the shaking and sonication steps, and simply replacing methanol with ethanol did not give a stable improvement. I then changed the extraction solvent to phosphate buffer/methanol (50:50) and tested different pH values. At pH 6, the average recovery was 86.16% for glycyrrhizic acid and 92.43% for aspartame. Both were within the 70–120% range used in this study. In a separate method comparison, glycyrrhizic acid recovery increased from 9.99% with the original standard method to 87.05% with the selected condition in the plain steamed bun control. The two results used the same spike level and recovery calculation. The results across different matrices also showed that improving one target compound in one matrix does not mean that all compounds and food matrices will perform the same. Method suitability must be judged from the full validation results.

My goal was to find sample preparation conditions that improved glycyrrhizic acid recovery without causing a clear drop in recovery for the other target sweeteners.

A | Identify the Cause: Matrix Effect or Extraction Efficiency?

I first confirmed that glycyrrhizic acid recovery was low and ruled out the instrument as the main cause. So, instead of continuing to adjust the instrument, I focused on whether the low recovery was more likely caused by a matrix effect or poor extraction efficiency.

Results after centrifugation, filtration, and final volume adjustment moved closer to the target, so I did not treat filtration or final volume adjustment as the main improvement targets. This identifies the main loss stage, but it does not prove one single chemical mechanism.

The peak area from the residue after the third extraction was 25,327. Without correcting for volume, matrix, and response differences, I treated this only as evidence that some signal remained in the residue, not as a direct measure of mass recovery.

For this direct comparison, the spike level and recovery calculation were confirmed to be the same. These results are not combined with exploratory data from other dates.

My decision:In the direct comparison using the same spike level and recovery calculation, glycyrrhizic acid recovery in plain steamed bun increased from 9.99% with the original method to 87.05% with the candidate condition. This is an increase of 77.06 percentage points, or about 8.71 times the original value. Cross-matrix validation showed that glycyrrhizic acid recovery was around 80–90% in most tested matrices, but it was still below 70% in dried meat. Reb-B recovery remained high in several matrices. My conclusion is that the candidate condition clearly improved recovery in plain steamed bun, and pH 6 is a more balanced candidate. However, the method's suitable range must still be defined by validation results for different matrices and analytes. Some issues remain unresolved.

My Technical Value

01

Identify the Type of Problem First

I did not immediately blame the instrument for the low recovery. I first separated possible matrix effects from extraction efficiency.

02

Use Each Experiment to Guide the Next Step

The pre- and post-extraction spike tests shifted my focus to extraction. Step-by-step spike tests then helped locate the largest difference at the extraction stage.

03

Make Decisions Based on Results and Limits

I compared different extraction conditions and results from multiple analytes, then used cross-matrix validation to check the limits of the candidate condition.

My value is not only in improving recovery. It is also in making each experiment answer a clear question and using the evidence to decide what is most useful to test next.
Data limits:This report separates experimental results from my decisions. The matrix dilution section shows data only. After each experiment, the decision note explains how the results changed my next step. I have not guessed any missing peak area, CV, or instrument assignment.

Data and Conclusions

  • From 9.99% to 87.05%: This is a direct comparison in plain steamed bun under confirmed comparable conditions; it is not combined with data from other batches.
  • Consistent Matrix Source: The matrix used during this method optimization came from the same source of plain steamed bun.
  • Systematic Troubleshooting: I used a systematic decision tree to break down the problem instead of trying changes at random.
  • Locate the Main Loss: Step-by-step spike tests showed that the main loss occurred during extraction.
  • Balance Multiple Analytes: I considered recovery across multiple analytes when evaluating the trade-offs of method improvement.
  • Keep the Limitations Clear: This case supports extraction as the main loss stage and pH 6 as a more balanced candidate condition, but some issues remain unresolved.
